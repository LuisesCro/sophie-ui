# How to Use This Book

You have questions about the Mormons. This book has answers — one hundred of them.

Maybe a reality show or a social feed made you curious. Maybe you have Latter-day Saint neighbors, coworkers, in-laws, or friends and you'd rather understand them than guess. Maybe two young missionaries knocked on your door and you realized you had no idea what they actually believe. Whatever brought you here, you have specific questions, and you want clear, honest, jargon-free answers — not a sermon, and not an attack.

That's exactly what this book is built to give you.

Inside, you'll find the **one hundred questions people most often ask about Mormonism**, each answered in a page or two of plain, fair, genuinely useful English. The questions are grouped into ten themed sections — the basics, beliefs, the Book of Mormon, history, worship, temples, daily-life rules, family and gender, missions and money, and the hard modern questions — so you can read straight through or jump to whatever you're wondering about right now.

A few promises about the answers:

- **They're honest.** Where the answer is simple, you'll get it straight. Where believers and critics genuinely disagree — about history, about doctrine, about the hard chapters — this book tells you so, and lays out both sides fairly, rather than pretending there's no debate.
- **They're respectful.** Taking people seriously enough to describe their beliefs accurately is not the same as agreeing with them. This book never mocks and never preaches. It simply explains.
- **They're self-contained.** You don't need any background. Each answer stands on its own, so you can open to any page and understand it.
- **They bust the myths.** A lot of what "everyone knows" about Mormons is wrong — the polygamy, the underwear, the coffee, the horns. You'll leave able to tell fact from folklore.

One distinction is worth flagging before you start, because it clears up more confusion than any other: **the mainstream Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints is not the same as the polygamous "fundamentalist" groups you see in the news.** The mainstream church ended polygamy in 1890 and excommunicates anyone who practices it today. When a lurid headline says "Mormon," it's almost always describing a breakaway sect the mainstream church rejects. Keep that in your back pocket; it makes the whole subject click into focus.

So — what would you like to know first? Turn to the section that's calling your name, or just start at question one. Either way, by the end you'll understand the Latter-day Saints better than almost everyone around you.

Let's begin.
