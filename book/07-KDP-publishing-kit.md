## 1. The SEO thesis (why this listing wins organically)

The niche's highest-intent buyer keywords are **question-shaped explainer phrases** with real search volume and **near-zero title density (TD 0–1)** — meaning almost no competing book puts these exact phrases in its title. We target that gap. We deliberately do **not** try to rank for the scripture itself ("book of mormon," 4,281/mo but saturated and not our product) or for trademarked pop-culture terms (show/celebrity names — prohibited by KDP and legally risky).

**Strategy:** the **title** owns the fat-head terms (*Mormons / Latter-day Saints*), the **subtitle** absorbs the top question phrases (*are mormons christian, beliefs, history, everyday life*), and the **7 backend keyword fields** mop up the remaining long-tail (*lds books, ex-mormon/leaving, book of mormon explained, polygamy/FLDS, temple/garments, missionaries, comparative religion*). Together they blanket the niche's entire question-space.

## 2. Title & subtitle (the single most important SEO decision)

**Recommended (SEO-optimal, KDP-safe):**

> **Title:** Mormons: The Latter-day Saints Explained
> **Subtitle:** A Clear, Fair Guide to Mormon Beliefs, History, and Everyday Life — Are Mormons Christian, Who They Really Are, and What They Believe

- Leads with **"Mormons"** (the single highest-volume niche search term) while immediately using the respectful preferred name **"Latter-day Saints"** — signaling both discoverability and credibility.
- Subtitle captures, in natural language, the top TD-0 phrases: *mormon beliefs, history, everyday life, are mormons christian, what they believe.*
- No trademarks, no brand/show/celebrity names, no quality claims ("best," "#1") — all of which KDP prohibits in titles.

**Alternative A (if you prefer leading with the respectful name):**
> The Latter-day Saints, Explained — A Clear, Fair Guide to Mormon Beliefs, History & Everyday Life: Who Mormons Are, What They Believe, and Whether They're Christian

**Alternative B (explainer-forward):**
> Mormonism Explained — Latter-day Saint Beliefs, History, and Everyday Life Made Clear: Answers to the Questions People Actually Ask About Mormons

> ⚠️ **KDP rule:** the title/subtitle you upload must be *identical* to what appears on the cover. Keep metadata and cover text in sync, or the book can be blocked at review.

**Keyword compliance notes (KDP):** no other authors' or books' titles; no brand names or trademarks (no show, celebrity, or company names); no "bestseller/best/free" or subjective claims; no misleading or irrelevant terms. All seven above are descriptive, truthful, and relevant to the actual content. ✅

## 9. Cover brief (for your designer / AI tool)

- **Vibe:** calm, credible, trade-nonfiction — think a serious-but-accessible explainer, not a devotional and not a tabloid exposé.
- **Imagery options (original art only):** a stylized temple spire silhouette; the angel-Moroni trumpet motif rendered generically; or clean typographic cover with a single symbolic element. **Avoid** real photographs of identifiable temples/people (rights issues) and any official church logos/trademarks.
- **Title hierarchy:** "MORMONS" large, "The Latter-day Saints Explained" as the anchor, subtitle smaller beneath.
- **Legibility at thumbnail** is the #1 rule — it must read on a phone-sized search result.
- Must not imply official church endorsement.

## 10. Review & launch plan (organic ranking flywheel)

1. **Pre-launch:** line up an ARC (advance reader) team for honest reviews on day one.
2. **Launch week:** concentrate sales + KU reads to grab the "#1 New Release" banner in the small Mormonism node.
3. **Reviews:** end matter already asks readers for an honest review (see back matter). Aim for early velocity; never buy or incentivize reviews (KDP ban).
4. **Ads (optional):** Amazon Sponsored Products on the exact TD-0 keywords above (*are mormons christian, mormon beliefs, lds books, how to leave the mormon church*) — CPCs are cheap ($0.30–$1.00) in this niche.
5. **Fairness = review insurance:** the manuscript's deliberately balanced tone is itself an SEO asset — it minimizes the 1-star "biased" reviews that sink partisan niche titles and depress ranking.
